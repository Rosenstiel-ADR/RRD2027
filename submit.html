Presentation formats

Oral Talk

Selective. Best for in-depth results. Open to students, postdocs, staff, and faculty.

Lightning & Flash Talk

Short standalone lightning talks, plus optional flash talks for poster presenters.

Poster

A poster in a dedicated viewing block, with an optional flash talk (encouraged). Open to everyone.

Guidelines

  • Length: 1,500 characters maximum, excluding title and author list.
  • Format: Plain text. No figures or equations in the abstract body.
  • Authors: List all authors with affiliations.
  • Format preference: Indicate your preferred type (oral talk, lightning talk, or poster). Poster presenters may add an optional flash talk (encouraged). Assignments may be adjusted based on capacity and fit.
  • Eligibility: Open across the Rosenstiel School and collaborating groups. Open to students, postdocs, staff, and faculty. The Faculty page covers the separate Meet the Faculty session, which requires no abstract.

What the form will ask

Since submission also registers you for the day, the form collects both your abstract and the logistics we'd otherwise ask at registration:

  • You: name, email, role (student / postdoc / staff / faculty), department.
  • Your abstract: title, author list, abstract body, subject area keywords.
  • Format: preferred format (oral talk, lightning talk, or poster, with optional flash talk for poster presenters).
  • Logistics: dietary restrictions, lunch headcount.
  • Consent: photo / video consent for Rosenstiel communications.

Research & Presentation Excellence

Awards.

A Tradition of Excellence

Four awards, by attendee vote.

Winners are chosen by attendee vote through the post-event survey and announced about two weeks after the event: people's choice, rising tide, best communication, and the cross-currents award for bridging disciplines.

People's Choice

Voted by attendees for the presentation that most captured the audience's attention.

Rising Tide

For the student or early-career researcher whose work and presentation best exemplify the promise of Rosenstiel science.

Best Communication

For the presentation that most clearly and engagingly brought complex science to a broad audience, in any format.

Cross-Currents Award

For the work that best bridges two departments or disciplines across the school.
